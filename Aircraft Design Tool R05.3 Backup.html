Aircraft Design Tool

Version 5.2| July 27th, 2026 | Victor R | Google Gemini was used to create this tool

Features

  • Rendering: Renders top, side and front view of a simplified model of your airplane so you can visualize the CG location, mean chord and the scale of your design.
  • Main math: Calculates your mass, CG, Tail sizing and more.
  • Extra math: Calculates your coefficient of lift and drag from a NACA 4 digit code. Also gives ballpark numbers for important speeds like stall, takeoff and cruise.
  • Variables and Equations: Use variables to drive equations and equations to drive properties (Can drive more than one thing with one equation). Want to automatically make the spar the same length as your wingspan? Your wingspan can drive the spar length!
  • Mass tracking spreadsheet: Keep track of all your masses in one place and compare to different configurations using mass profiles.
  • Graph Sweep: Want to know how a dependent variable changes as you change an independent one? Graph sweep can do that! This feature is the most unstable at the moment.
  • Import/Export: Use "Save JSON" and "Load". Export as "Save HTML" for a self-contained file (not compatible between versions).

Limitations

  • Math formulas limited to 3 nested brackets for simplicity.
  • Restricted to NACA airfoils in the airfoil-data.js file.
  • All components are treated as rectangular prisms.
  • Simplified aero calculations are based on empirical formulas, not simulation.
  • Constants in the variables page do not adjust to unit changes.

User Guide

  • Switch layouts using the top view buttons (Top, Side, Front, Graph Sweep, Variables) and resize panels using the grid grab bars.
  • Use the setting icon in the top right to change units, or customize the appearance of the tool.
  • Managing Parts: Use the + Add button in the Parts panel to insert a component, click any item to edit its dimensions, rotation, or mirroring, and use the eye icon to hide/show items. You can also click and drag on the left side of a component's label to re-order it
  • Tracking Weights: Toggle between mass profiles using the top-right preset tabs, or open the spreadsheet table to compare columns side-by-side and export to CSV. To compare, click the down arrow and pick a profile. The columns can also be sorted by pressing the solid triangle icon.
  • Setting Up Equations: Open the Variables tab, create a variable or driven property, click the target button to use the eyedropper, and pick an input box to link them. Maximum of 3 nested brackets and symbols like +, -, *, /.
  • Running Sweeps: Head to Graph Sweep, pick a Y-axis target, add X-axis variables using the eyedropper crosshair to select a textbox. Define your bounds, and click Refresh to plot the curve. A jagged curve is due to the calculator rounding outputs.
  • Saving Work: Use Save JSON for a quick project file or Save HTML to pack the entire application into a single executable webpage. Saving as JSON allows you to transfer your design from one version of the design tool to a newer one.

Details

  • Center of Gravity: Calculated using the standard weighted mass moment sum: Xcg = Σ(mi × xi) / Σmi. Since parts are treated as solid rectangular blocks, their individual CGs are just dead center in their bounding boxes.
  • Tail Volume Coefficients: Uses classic empirical ratios. Horizontal volume (Vh) is (Sh × Lh) / (Sw × MAC). Vertical volume (Vv) is (Sv × Lv) / (Sw × b). Moment arms span from the main wing's aerodynamic center to the tail's aerodynamic center.
  • Aerodynamics & XFOIL: The tool parses your NACA 4-digit code to grab geometric specs. Lift (CL) and drag (CD) coefficients are pulled straight from a pre-baked XFOIL dataset (airfoil-data.js) for realistic viscous polars.
  • Drag Estimation: Built using the standard buildup method. Induced drag (CDi) uses the classic lifting-line approximation: CL2 / (π × AR × e), where Oswald efficiency (e) is dynamically estimated using Raymer's approximation. Parasitic drag uses the baseline Cd0 you select in the properties panel.
  • Flight Speeds: Stall speed (Vs) comes directly from steady level flight equilibrium: √(2W / (ρ × S × CLmax)) at standard sea level. Optimal cruise speed is calculated by iterating through the 3D polar to find the exact angle of attack that yields maximum L/D.
  • Geometric Constraints: Wing sweep and taper math assume perfectly straight leading and trailing edges. Complex aero-like fuselage lift, wing-root downwash on the tail, and dynamic stability is ignored.
  • Wing Volume: Calculated using a 3D frustum (pyramid with top chopped off) formula to accurately account for root and tip taper. Sweep is ignored since shearing a shape does not alter its total volume. Instead of true integration, the cross-section uses the max thickness from your NACA code and applies a ~68.3% teardrop approximation factor to the bounding box. If hollowed, it subtracts a smaller internal frustum based on your wall thickness.
  • Graph Sweep Engine: Steps your chosen independent variable through defined bounds, forcing a recalculation of the whole equation web at every step to plot the points. Once done, it snaps everything back to its original state.
  • What da heck is this giant HTML file?: The tool is a single HTML file running plain JavaScript. I decided to do this so its easy to share and run. Kinda crazy to scroll through the code though.
  • Mass Discrepancies: Treating everything as uniform blocks ignores taper, hollowing, and point masses. If your real build has complex internal structures, your actual CG will definitely drift from this calculation.
  • Aerodynamic Oversights: While the tool does account for finite span effects and induced downwash based on your Aspect Ratio, it ignores complex 3D interference. Wing-root downwash on the tail, fuselage lift, tip vortices, and dynamic stability are excluded. Aero is a mathematical approximation.
  • Software buggy-ness: Graph Sweep is the most buggy feature. Rapid updates can cause jagged plots due to rounding. Also, the variable parser has no cyclic protection, if two variables drive each other, the app will freeze. I also tested this with the fewest crazy scenarios.
